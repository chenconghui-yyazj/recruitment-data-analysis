# 上市公司招聘大数据：人才需求变化分析与就业趋势研判（分析报告 · English / 英文版）

> Based on listed-company recruitment data (2014–2026, cleaned dataset: 323,818 records × 10 fields), this report identifies the structure and evolution of talent demand across six dimensions: industry, city, education, job category, salary, and time.

---

## 1. Background & Objectives

Listed-company recruitment information is a key data element reflecting regional economic vitality, industrial-structure change, and enterprise talent demand. By cleaning, mining, and analyzing recruitment data, we can identify talent-demand characteristics across industries, regions, and roles, and reveal employment-market trends to support **university talent cultivation, student career guidance, and enterprise talent allocation**.

This project addresses three competition tasks:
1. **Data Cleaning & Governance** — build a high-quality analyzable dataset;
2. **Demand Feature Analysis** — characterize multi-dimensional structure (industry, region, education, job, time);
3. **Hot Jobs & Demand Change** — identify trends over time.

## 3. Key Findings

### 3.1 Industry Structure: Software & Electronics Lead

| Rank | Industry | Job postings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Software and IT services | 44,352 |
| 2 | Computer, communication & other electronic equipment mfg | 41,331 |
| 3 | Electrical machinery & equipment mfg | 23,177 |
| 4 | Pharmaceutical manufacturing | 17,325 |
| 5 | Real estate | 14,972 |

**Finding**: Software & IT services and computer/electronic equipment manufacturing lead by a wide margin; digital economy and high-end manufacturing form the main force of current talent demand. Pharmaceuticals and electrical machinery follow, reflecting industrial upgrading and expansion in people's-livelihood industries.

### 3.2 Regional Distribution: Significant First-tier City Siphon Effect

| Rank | City | Job postings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Beijing | 36,293 |
| 2 | Shanghai | 30,137 |
| 3 | Shenzhen | 26,296 |
| 4 | Hangzhou | 17,028 |
| 5 | Guangzhou | 14,948 |

**Finding**: Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Hangzhou, and Guangzhou form the first tier of talent demand, far exceeding new first-tier cities. First-tier cities and industrial agglomerations remain the core of listed-company talent deployment.

### 3.3 Education Requirements: Bachelor & Associate Dominate

| Education | Postings | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bachelor | 145,370 | ~44.9% |
| Associate | 105,060 | ~32.4% |
| Open | 36,053 | ~11.1% |
| Other | 29,862 | ~9.2% |
| Master | 7,080 | ~2.2% |
| PhD | 393 | ~0.1% |

**Finding**: Bachelor and Associate together account for ~77%, the absolute majority of listed-company hiring. Postgraduate demand is small and concentrated in high-precision fields — applied talents are in high demand.

### 3.4 Salary Level: Overall Average & High-Paying Industries

- Overall average monthly salary ≈ **9,104 CNY**.
- Top 5 high-paying industries (sample ≥ 30): Monetary finance (11,525), Telecom/broadcast & satellite transmission services (11,266), Sports (10,925), Internet services (10,840), Building installation (10,821).

**Finding**: Knowledge-intensive & modern service industries (finance, telecom, internet) lead in salary; traditional manufacturing has a large base but flatter average — salary is highly correlated with industry added value.

### 3.5 Job Demand: Technical & General Roles Coexist

| Job category | Postings |
| --- | --- |
| Technical | 7,311 |
| Sales / Customer service / Tech support | 5,372 |
| Finance / HR / Admin | 4,452 |
| Pharmaceutical / Medical | 3,902 |
| IT / Internet | 3,714 |

**Finding**: Technical roles rank first; combined with IT/Internet categories, digital roles further rise, confirming a widening tech-talent gap under digital transformation. General functional roles (sales, finance, admin) remain stable — a "technical-first, functional-support" structure.

---

## 4. Trend Analysis

- **Industry structure drives demand**: digital economy (software, internet, IT) and high-end manufacturing (electronics, electrical, pharma) keep expanding — the core engine of job-post growth.
- **Talent agglomerates in high-tier cities**: first & new first-tier cities show a clear siphon effect; the job market is spatially polarized.
- **Large applied-talent gap**: the bachelor/associate-dominant structure shows enterprises value practical, hands-on talent; postgraduate demand is concentrated in R&D roles.
- **Role structure upgrading**: technical/digital roles lead, reflecting rising requirements for digital capability and composite skills.

---

## 5. Applications & Recommendations

**For universities**: strengthen digital-economy majors (software, electronic information, data analysis) and add applied-skill courses to match the "technical + applied" hiring orientation.

**For students**: first & new first-tier cities offer more opportunities — prioritize digital roles and build hard skills (programming, data analysis) early.

**For enterprises**: the tech-talent gap is large; expand campus & technical hiring and improve salary incentives; watch growth industries like pharma and electronics.

**For government / industrial parks**: align industry and talent policy, introduce talent-attraction & cultivation policies for high-demand industries (software, electronics) to ease structural supply-demand imbalances.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Through multi-dimensional analysis of 323,818 listed-company job records, this project systematically characterizes talent-demand structure across industry, region, education, job, and salary: **digital economy & high-end manufacturing lead demand, first-tier cities concentrate talent, bachelor/associate dominate hiring, and technical roles have the largest gap**. The findings support university cultivation, student career planning, enterprise allocation, and policymaking. The analysis process and visualization output are fully open-sourced, reproducible, and traceable.
